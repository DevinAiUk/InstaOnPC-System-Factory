# InstaOnPC System Factory — Operator Tutorial

*Create a client-ready Local Lead Engine in under 15 minutes, then review it responsibly.*

## How to use this tutorial

This tutorial follows the exact page order in the System Factory workspace. Use a fictional or approved client project while learning. The application creates drafts; it does not activate workflows, publish content, send outreach, or write to outside business systems.

- Work left to right: intake, evidence, diagnosis, recommendation, assets, launch, reporting, export.
- Approve only facts you can trace to a client, source, or direct observation.
- Lock confirmed facts before regenerating assets.
- Treat every exported package as draft material until the client and operator review it.
## Fast operator workflow

### 1. Dashboard

Purpose: See active projects, prepared assets, review status, verified client runs, and the next action across the workspace.

- Open the dashboard and check the active-project count.
- Use Recent work to identify the project with the clearest next action.
- Choose New project when the business is not already listed.
- Do not count fictional demos as verified client runs.
### 2. Projects

Purpose: Search and filter every business workspace without mixing demos and real client work.

- Search by business name, city, or industry.
- Filter between all projects, fictional demos, and client projects.
- Open the project whose owner, objective, and pipeline stage you intend to work on.
- Confirm the project identity before editing any facts or assets.
### 3. New project

Purpose: Create the smallest complete intake needed to generate a tailored Local Lead Engine.

- Enter the business name, industry, city, website, and service area.
- Add the actual services offered and select one primary objective.
- Record the owner-reported bottleneck; do not rewrite an assumption as a fact.
- List current tools, booking method, CRM, lead sources, contact channels, FAQs, proof, and compliance boundaries.
- Review all four intake steps before creating the project.
### 4. Project overview

Purpose: Check delivery readiness and enter the next stage of the selected project.

- Confirm the objective and pain statement at the top.
- Review the recommended-opportunity score, approved assets, and approved sources.
- Use the six module cards to move directly to the highest-value task.
- Treat the readiness percentage as workflow completion, not proof of business results.
### 5. Business intake

Purpose: Edit the source profile after a client clarification or operator review.

- Update only fields that have genuinely changed or were previously missing.
- Leave uncertain pricing, service areas, compliance details, and technology choices blank or labeled for review.
- Respect locked fields; unlock only when the underlying fact has been reverified.
- Save the intake, then regenerate only assets affected by the changed facts.
### 6. Business intelligence

Purpose: Build an evidence layer before generating persuasive copy or automation logic.

- Review the business profile and mark missing CRM, booking, website, or service information.
- Use the public website scanner only for a permitted public HTTPS page.
- Read every source entry before approving it.
- Add approved client notes or source material with an optional source URL.
- Lock confirmed fields such as business name, city, and phone number.
- After approving evidence, regenerate the affected content so its source references update.
### 7. Revenue-leak audit

Purpose: Trace friction across Discover, Inquire, Respond, Qualify, and Hand off.

- Read the observation, evidence label, severity, recommended action, and required human input for every leak.
- Separate what the client reported from what the operator inferred.
- Prioritize leaks that affect a real conversion event such as a call, estimate request, consultation, or booking.
- Continue only when the human-review requirement is understood.
### 8. Opportunity board

Purpose: Compare at least three solutions with a consistent 100-point scoring model.

- Review the weighted score: urgency 25%, financial value 20%, frequency 15%, access 15%, delivery feasibility 15%, evidence quality 10%.
- Open Reasoning & assumptions for each option.
- Check required inputs, risks, dependencies, human review, measurement definition, and first action.
- Adjust a dimension only when you have a defensible reason.
- Choose one primary package; the score is decision support, not a promised outcome.
### 9. Offer builder

Purpose: Turn the selected opportunity into a focused, client-readable scope.

- Confirm the promise is framed as a business outcome without guarantees.
- Review what is included, what is excluded, what the client must provide, and what requires approval.
- Edit owner and next action before saving a version.
- Resolve safeguard warnings before approval.
- Lock the module after sign-off so unrelated regeneration cannot overwrite it.
### 10. System builder

Purpose: Assemble the tailored operating system and review all 23 generated assets.

- Check the four-part architecture: Capture, Qualify, Review, Hand off.
- Review the selected system name against the approved opportunity.
- Open individual assets rather than regenerating everything by default.
- Use Regenerate unlocked modules only after a material intake or strategy change.
- Select planned integrations as design choices; every listed connector remains a mock until separately implemented and verified.
### 11. Skill studio

Purpose: Edit and version the eight business-specific Manus-style skills.

- Review opportunity mapper, prospect audit generator, AI lead-system architect, knowledge-base builder, content humanizer, GEO/AEO writer, automation implementation brief, and performance reporter.
- Confirm each skill has purpose, inputs, tasks, outputs, rules, safety limits, evidence labels, and a handoff.
- Save a new version after substantive edits.
- Approve only after QA warnings are cleared and locked facts remain unchanged.
- Lock completed skills before regenerating sibling modules.
### 12. Workflow studio

Purpose: Convert the strategy into an inactive, implementation-ready specification.

- Follow the diagram from signed intake through validation, approved knowledge, and reviewed handoff.
- Verify the trigger, field map, consent, disclosure, deduplication, idempotency, failure handling, retry limits, audit logging, and test cases.
- Confirm every external destination and field ID before implementation.
- Do not treat a Make, n8n, or Zapier specification as an import-tested live blueprint.
- Require a payload-bound human approval before any future external write.
### 13. Outreach studio

Purpose: Produce calm, business-specific outreach based on verified observations.

- Confirm the prospect criteria match the business type, service area, and objective.
- Choose the desired voice, then regenerate the selected message.
- Use one relevant observation, one practical idea, and one low-pressure call to action.
- Never infer consent from a public phone number or social profile.
- Review and approve the draft, but send it from a separate authorized process.
### 14. Content studio

Purpose: Turn approved source material into reusable content atoms and channel drafts.

- Check that each draft points to approved source material.
- Search and select the exact content asset you want to edit.
- Humanize the draft while preserving names, prices, numbers, links, keywords, quotations, and uncertainty.
- Regenerate only the affected asset.
- Approve content only after a person verifies its claims and channel fit.
### 15. Local visibility

Purpose: Improve service answers, FAQs, local relevance, and conversion paths without claiming rankings.

- Review service-and-city language for factual accuracy.
- Confirm business address, service area, phone, review data, and service claims before use.
- Improve clear next actions and answer-style FAQs.
- Do not claim a missing social URL means the business has no account.
- Treat recommendations as tests to validate, not ranking guarantees.
### 16. Launch hub

Purpose: Assign a staged 30/60/90-day roadmap and document accountable owners.

- In the first phase, establish the baseline, verify assets, and run controlled tests.
- In the second phase, refine approved content and prepare integrations.
- In the third phase, compare results and recommend the next bounded improvement.
- Assign a real owner to every task and mark completion only after evidence exists.
- Record a successful manual client run only for a real client with approved assets, completed first-phase QA, measurements, and sign-off.
### 17. Reporting hub

Purpose: Record truthful baselines and measured results without overstating attribution.

- Enter the metric, baseline, current value, unit, comparable periods, and measurement source.
- Use the same definition and time basis for baseline and current values.
- Explain observed change separately from causation.
- Refresh the reporting narrative after measurements are saved.
- Recommend one measurable next test rather than a broad promise.
### 18. Export center

Purpose: Prepare a reviewable client package with integrity checks and clear boundaries.

- Read the draft-package acknowledgment before downloading.
- Review approved assets, outstanding QA issues, and the SHA-256 integrity status.
- Confirm the seven-folder model: skills, workspace, automation, baseline, documentation, portfolio, exports.
- Download the ZIP or selected Markdown, CSV, Make specification, or workspace JSON.
- Use Print / save PDF for the client-readable offer, roadmap, and reporting plan.
- Inspect the manifest and checksums before delivery; no Drive, Docs, Notion, CRM, publishing, or workflow activation occurs automatically.
### 19. Workspace settings

Purpose: Understand operating mode, pricing references, safety defaults, and deployment boundaries.

- Confirm the workspace is in review-led draft mode.
- Keep credentials in a server-side secret manager; never put them in generated skills or exports.
- Use a private access password and durable storage before hosting.
- Treat the PostgreSQL schema and live adapters as future integration work.
- Complete three verified manual client runs before expanding beyond the local-service-business MVP.
## End-to-end quality gate

- The business identity and objective are correct.
- Required facts are approved, and important facts are locked.
- Revenue leaks are labeled by evidence type.
- One opportunity is selected after assumptions and risks are reviewed.
- All client-facing claims are supportable and non-guaranteed.
- Skills and assets have owners, next actions, versions, and approval states.
- Workflow field maps, consent, failure handling, and tests are complete.
- Content has approved source references and passed humanization review.
- Roadmap tasks have owners and measurable completion evidence.
- Reporting compares like-for-like periods and does not imply causality.
- The export manifest, folder structure, and checksums have been inspected.
- The client understands the package is draft/import-ready, not live.
## Troubleshooting

### A draft cannot be approved

Open the QA issue list, remove unsupported promises or credential-like text, complete missing workflow mappings, and ensure content has approved source references.

### A fact will not change

The field is locked. Verify the corrected value first, unlock the fact intentionally, update the intake, then relock it.

### Website scanning fails

Confirm the site uses public HTTPS and is reachable from the deployment. The scanner does not follow redirects, execute JavaScript, access protected pages, inspect social profiles, or scan private addresses. Paste approved source text when direct capture is unavailable.

### An integration says Mock

That is expected in the MVP. The app can prepare specifications and payloads, but a live adapter requires separately managed credentials, destination discovery, payload review, explicit approval, idempotency, verification, and redacted audit logging.
