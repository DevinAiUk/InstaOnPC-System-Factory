*A practical curriculum for building, reviewing, and packaging Local Lead Engines for lead-driven local businesses.*

## Course purpose

This course prepares an InstaOnPC operator to turn a structured business intake into a client-ready Local Lead Engine package while preserving evidence, human approval, data safety, and truthful reporting. Completion demonstrates safe use of the current single-operator MVP; it does not certify live CRM, publishing, outreach, or workflow deployment.

## Audience and prerequisites

- Audience: InstaOnPC operators, implementation partners, project managers, and reviewers.
- Prerequisite: working knowledge of local-business sales funnels and basic familiarity with websites, CRM, booking, SEO, and automation.
- Training environment: use one of the fictional demo projects or an approved internal test case.
- Production boundary: do not enter secrets or activate external systems during training.
## Certification outcomes

By the end of the course, the learner can:

- Create a complete, evidence-aware business intake.
- Diagnose revenue leaks across the customer journey.
- Apply the weighted opportunity model and explain a recommendation.
- Edit, version, approve, lock, and selectively regenerate factory assets.
- Review an inactive workflow specification for security and reliability.
- Create humanized outreach and local content grounded in approved sources.
- Plan a measured 30/60/90-day implementation.
- Export and quality-check a client delivery package without implying it is live.
## Course format

- Eight instructional modules.
- One guided lab per module.
- Short knowledge checks throughout.
- One capstone client simulation.
- One operator review checklist and certification rubric.
## Module 1 — Factory orientation and operating boundaries

### Learning objectives

- Explain what the System Factory produces.
- Distinguish drafts, approved assets, import-ready specifications, and live systems.
- Navigate the dashboard, projects list, project overview, and settings.
### Lesson

The System Factory is a review-led operator workspace. It turns intake and approved sources into an offer, audit, eight skills, workflow specifications, outreach, content, a roadmap, reporting assets, and an export bundle. Its primary MVP is the Local Lead Engine for appointment and service businesses. It uses deterministic mock generation and blocks external execution.

- Dashboard: workspace-wide status and next actions.
- Projects: searchable separation of demos and clients.
- Project overview: readiness, selected opportunity, source approvals, and module entry points.
- Settings: service scope, safety boundaries, storage, access, and mock integration status.
### Guided lab

Open a fictional demo. Identify its primary objective, completion status, highest-ranked opportunity, number of approved assets, and number of approved sources. Explain why none of those values proves real business impact.

### Knowledge check

- What does an approved draft authorize?
- Why does a fictional demo never count as a verified client run?
- Which page shows workspace operating boundaries?
## Module 2 — Intake, evidence, and fact locks

### Learning objectives

- Capture the minimum viable business profile.
- Separate user-provided facts, sourced facts, inferences, recommendations, and needs-review items.
- Use source approvals and fact locks correctly.
### Lesson

A useful system begins with a narrow conversion event and reliable inputs. The intake captures identity, location, services, objective, tools, proof, and constraints. Business Intelligence holds the source ledger and immutable fact controls. A public scan is an optional input, not an authority by itself.

- Required start: business name, industry, city, service list, and primary objective.
- Recommended detail: service area, booking method, CRM, contact channels, lead sources, FAQs, pricing guidance, proof, compliance notes, address, phone, review data, and social URLs.
- Approval rule: read the exact source before approving it.
- Lock rule: lock confirmed identity and operational facts before regeneration.
- Change rule: unlock only after the replacement value is verified.
### Guided lab

Create a training project for a Tampa Bay home-service business. Add one explicit owner-reported pain point and one clearly labeled needs-review field. Approve one training source and lock the business name and city.

### Knowledge check

- Why is a public social profile not proof of contact consent?
- When should a fact remain unlocked?
- What must happen after a newly approved source affects existing content?
## Module 3 — Revenue-leak diagnosis and opportunity scoring

### Learning objectives

- Trace friction from discovery through handoff.
- Use the six-factor weighted score consistently.
- Explain assumptions, risks, inputs, and human-review needs.
### Lesson

The Revenue-Leak Audit organizes friction across Discover, Inquire, Respond, Qualify, and Hand off. The Opportunity Board then compares solutions with the same score: 25% urgency, 20% financial value, 15% frequency, 15% access to buyer or evidence, 15% delivery feasibility, and 10% evidence quality.

- Score the business problem, not the novelty of the technology.
- Prefer a narrow conversion event the client can observe.
- Keep evidence quality low when facts are incomplete.
- State required inputs and dependencies before recommending implementation.
- Never translate an opportunity score into a guaranteed return.
### Guided lab

Review all revenue leaks in the training project. Compare the three recommended opportunities, open each rationale, change one score only if new evidence supports it, and select the best primary package. Write a two-sentence explanation a client could understand.

### Knowledge check

- Which scoring factor carries the greatest weight?
- Why can a high-value idea still rank below a simpler solution?
- What is the difference between evidence quality and access?
## Module 4 — Offer design and system architecture

### Learning objectives

- Convert a selected opportunity into an outcome-led offer.
- Map Capture, Qualify, Review, and Hand off components.
- Choose integrations without claiming they are connected.
### Lesson

Offer Builder narrows the engagement to one understandable result, explicit inclusions and exclusions, required client inputs, ownership, and approvals. System Builder maps the connected parts and exposes the full asset set. Integration checkboxes are architecture preferences only; the current adapters do not write externally.

- Sell the business outcome rather than an AI tool.
- Preserve the approved scope and pricing guidance exactly.
- Make human escalation visible in qualification and handoff logic.
- Regenerate individual modules when only one area changes.
- Lock client-approved modules before larger regeneration.
### Guided lab

Edit the training offer so the scope is clear to a business owner. Review the four architecture stages. Set an owner and next action, save a version, resolve any safeguard warning, approve the offer, and lock it.

### Knowledge check

- What should happen when an inquiry is uncertain or sensitive?
- When is full-project regeneration appropriate?
- What does selecting HubSpot or Make in System Builder actually do?
## Module 5 — Skill Studio and knowledge assets

### Learning objectives

- Explain the purpose of all eight skill modules.
- Evaluate a skill for completeness and safe handoff.
- Use versions, approvals, and locks without losing edits.
### Lesson

- Opportunity mapper: defines the niche, bottleneck, conversion event, and offer.
- Prospect audit generator: produces personalized audits, Loom scripts, and draft outreach.
- AI lead-system architect: maps chatbot, voice, website, SEO, CRM, routing, and staged delivery.
- Knowledge-base builder: creates bot-ready Markdown and JSON structures.
- Content humanizer: improves naturalness while preserving factual literals and qualifiers.
- GEO/AEO writer: creates local service pages, FAQs, and answer-engine copy.
- Automation implementation brief: creates Make-ready, security-aware specifications.
- Client performance reporter: produces truthful performance narratives and optimization plans.
### Skill quality standard

- Purpose and trigger are clear.
- Inputs identify required and optional evidence.
- Tasks are ordered and bounded.
- Outputs follow a predictable contract.
- Rules prohibit unsupported claims, secrets, automatic outreach, publishing, and assumed integrations.
- The next-skill handoff is explicit.
### Guided lab

Open three different skills. Edit one instruction in the humanizer without changing protected facts. Save a new version, compare status, approve it, lock it, and regenerate a different unlocked skill to confirm the first edit remains.

### Knowledge check

- Which skill converts services, policies, objections, and FAQs into bot-ready structures?
- What literals must the humanizer preserve?
- Why should skills have an explicit handoff?
## Module 6 — Workflow, outreach, content, and local visibility

### Learning objectives

- Review an automation specification for completeness and safety.
- Create consultative outreach from verified observations.
- Humanize and approve source-linked content.
### Workflow review

- Trigger schema and consent are explicit.
- Required fields have exact source-to-destination mappings.
- Validation and deduplication occur before downstream work.
- Idempotency prevents duplicate external actions.
- Failures use bounded retries and route to a human.
- Sensitive data and credentials are excluded from generated assets.
- Tests include happy path, missing data, duplicate request, provider error, and approval revocation.
- Specification status remains inactive until separately implemented and verified.
### Outreach review

- Use a verified business observation.
- Match one or two InstaOnPC services to the observed need.
- Keep the tone professional, calm, and low pressure.
- Do not guarantee rankings, leads, appointments, revenue, or ROI.
- Do not send from inside the draft workspace.
### Content and visibility review

- Every content atom points to approved evidence.
- Service, city, pricing, address, phone, and review claims are verified.
- FAQs answer real customer questions in plain language.
- Humanization preserves exact claims, values, links, keywords, quotations, and certainty.
- Local visibility recommendations are framed as tests, not ranking guarantees.
### Guided lab

Review the training workflow field map and test cases. Rewrite one outreach draft in the warm and personable voice. Humanize one content asset and confirm every literal remains intact. Approve neither asset until its evidence references are valid.

### Knowledge check

- What makes a workflow specification implementation-ready but still inactive?
- Why is a public phone number not automatic outreach permission?
- What must remain unchanged during humanization?
## Module 7 — Launch planning and truthful reporting

### Learning objectives

- Assign a realistic 30/60/90-day roadmap.
- Define baselines and comparable measurement periods.
- Separate observed change from causal claims.
### Lesson

Launch Hub gives each task an owner and phase. Reporting Hub records baselines, current measurements, units, comparable periods, and sources. The product may calculate an observed percentage change, but the operator must avoid claiming the system caused that change without stronger evidence.

- First phase: establish evidence, QA, baseline, and a bounded manual test.
- Second phase: refine based on feedback and prepare approved connections.
- Third phase: compare results, document limitations, and choose the next test.
- A successful client run requires a real client, approved assets, completed first-phase QA, measurements, and client sign-off.
- Fictional projects never satisfy the successful-run gate.
### Guided lab

Assign owners to every training roadmap task. Enter a sample baseline and current measurement with the same unit and comparable periods. Write one sentence describing the observed change and one sentence explaining why causality remains unproven.

### Knowledge check

- What fields are required for a measured result?
- Why can a percentage change be truthful while a causal claim is not?
- What evidence is required for a successful manual client run?
## Module 8 — Export, handoff, and implementation readiness

### Learning objectives

- Run the final quality gate.
- Explain the seven-folder export model.
- Hand off a draft package without implying external deployment.
### Lesson

Export Center packages the reviewed work into skills, workspace, automation, baseline, documentation, portfolio, and exports folders. The ZIP includes a manifest and per-file SHA-256 checksums. Additional formats include Markdown, CSV, Make specification, workspace JSON, and a print-ready client document.

- Review approval counts and every remaining QA issue.
- Read and accept the draft-package acknowledgment.
- Download and inspect the package before client delivery.
- Confirm all eight skill files and shared controls are present.
- Verify the manifest says the package is inactive.
- Treat workflow files as specifications until native module IDs, connections, scopes, and tests are validated.
- Do not claim Drive, Docs, Notion, HubSpot, Make, n8n, Zapier, publishing, or outreach delivery occurred.
### Guided lab

Export the training project in ZIP, Markdown, CSV, Make specification, and workspace JSON formats. Inspect the folder structure, manifest, and checksums. Prepare a five-minute client handoff that clearly separates completed review work from future implementation.

### Knowledge check

- What does SHA-256 verify?
- What does an approved module not authorize?
- Which file confirms the active or inactive package status?
## Capstone — Build a Local Lead Engine

Create a complete training workspace for one approved local-service-business case. The learner must:

- Complete all four intake stages.
- Approve at least one legitimate source and lock core identity facts.
- Identify revenue leaks across the customer journey.
- Compare all three opportunities and defend the selected package.
- Review and approve an outcome-led offer.
- Edit and version at least two skills.
- Validate one workflow specification and its tests.
- Create one outreach draft and one humanized content asset from approved evidence.
- Assign the 30/60/90-day roadmap.
- Record one comparable measurement without claiming causality.
- Export the package and explain its safety boundaries.
## Certification rubric

### Pass standard

- Evidence discipline: facts and claims are correctly labeled, sourced, and locked.
- Strategic judgment: the selected offer addresses a specific conversion bottleneck.
- Implementation quality: assets, field maps, tests, ownership, and handoffs are complete.
- Safety: no unsupported promises, credentials, automatic outreach, publishing, or assumed live integrations.
- Communication: client-facing explanations are clear, natural, and non-technical where possible.
- Measurement: baselines, periods, sources, and limitations are explicit.
- Export integrity: the bundle structure, manifest, and checksums are verified.
### Remediation standard

If any safety, evidence, approval, or export-integrity criterion fails, the learner revises the affected module and repeats only that verification section. A polished appearance cannot compensate for an unsupported claim or missing approval.
